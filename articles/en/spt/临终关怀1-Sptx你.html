Hospice 1-Sptx you

Sptx you, ooc count me

...is sptneo and I think he seems to be out of options. This is probably really short (right?)

——

You pressed your slightly flattened stomach. Just moments ago, you were still sad about what to eat for lunch, and now you could hear the sound of your stomach rumbling.

…… Actually, if you want to eat something, it's not that you can't afford to buy delicacies, but you're not interested in those foods. Most people even feel nauseous after a few bites, and the effort they finally eat is simply to maintain their own physiological functions.

So, you decided to take a different path! You don't want to torture your body anymore, so start changing today! You decide to try different ingredients—maybe they'll make a difference......

Now, you decide to pick up a corpse and taste it first. After all, no one wants a corpse. If it's the kind with no relatives, that would be even better—the kind you like. And if the corpse were stewed, it would probably taste great. You guessed, but you guessed based on the aroma of cooked meat you normally smelled, since you couldn't eat it anyway. Anyway, just thinking about it now makes you crave it.

Wiping the drool from the corner of your mouth, you decide to wander around aimlessly; maybe you’ll stumble upon a corpse in some secluded corner. You’ve heard that the basement of the Queen’s mansion is a bit like a dungeon, secretive and mysterious, probably holding many corpses—though you don’t know why a normally tightly sealed basement is now so openly accessible. That doesn’t stop you from running wildly to the basement to look for new ingredients.

Unfortunately, you feel disappointed, because apart from the empty walls and the mysterious tracks, there's nothing else in the basement—oh, if the three people standing there and the guy lying on the ground count as anything too.

…… Wait, the one lying there? Could it be a corpse? Suddenly, you became interested, feeling a bit nervous and anticipating as you walked over, ignoring the sword raised behind you and the wary gaze. You just squatted down and looked around—hmm, it seemed dead...... It felt like your heart was beating no more. So you lifted the corpse up satisfied, humming a tune that was completely out of tune, and walked toward the basement door.

…… It's this corpse monster with gritted teeth. You have to think about whether these iron pieces are edible or if you have to peel them all off like skinning...... You were seriously pondering this question, completely unaware that your legs on your shoulders trembled slightly.

Until you hear a slightly electric cracking sound and a goosebump-inducing sound like iron scraping against each other: "How long will ...... [you] continue to [drag and pull]?" ”

You hesitate for two seconds. Your first reaction is shock and extreme disappointment: this corpse isn’t completely dead! Which means you can’t eat it directly! Your second reaction is to console yourself... even though it’s not fully dead, it seems close to dying; maybe one day later it’ll finally become a corpse, so using it as reserve food shouldn’t be too bad.

Later, you come to realize—oh, because this is a corpse, when being dragged, its head and half its body were dragging on the ground, occasionally bumping into stones along the way.

"Uh, I don't know, but anyway, Mr. Corpse, you probably can't walk on your own. Should I just throw this for you?" You said casually, but since the corpse is alive and can still talk, you do have a subtle conscience. So you turn around, first throw those two legs to the ground with a clang, then pick up his head and sit it on your shoulder. Finally, you nod in satisfaction. Hmm, that's good. Although now it's the lower body suffering, you can only add a light apology in your heart, without any regrets.

“…… First, I am not a corpse now. ”

As soon as he finished speaking, you quickly added, dragging him forward: "If you're not right now, you will be in the future." ”

…… He was choked by you, and after a while, things quieted between you and you. You didn't pay much attention. He didn't say no, so you planned to drag him back.

"...... in the future, it might not be a [corpse]!" His voice sounded somewhat annoyed.

And you just sighed with a hint of regret: "What? If it's not a corpse, then it turns into charcoal? That probably doesn't taste good...... But roasted wood should be edible too? Forget it, Mr. Corpse, you'd better not die yet—it's my reserve food. "You added another sentence at the end, not very serious."

“…… Stop calling me that, I have a [name]. ”

You scratched your head. To be honest, you didn't really care what Mr. Corpse's name was called. You didn't care much about the name of a corpse reserve food that was destined to die and probably turn into food. But you decided to brush it off and treat it as hospice care.

So you casually asked, "...... Alright, then what's your name? ”

“Spamton.G.Spamton,” you hear him say. Honestly, probably because his vocal cords are vibrating, you feel occasional tremors along your back—quite uncomfortable. You almost want to leave him here and pick him up once he’s completely dead... but what if someone else takes your reserve food? Thinking it over, you decide to grit your teeth and overcome the difficulty to bring him back.

"Oh, okay, Mr. Corpse, I understand." You nodded obediently, using your previous form of address. Actually, you hadn't even heard it earlier, and naturally didn't remember what his name was.

“…… I [seriously emphasize], I have [name].' "It sounded like his voice was tinged with fatigue.

Alright, you have to slightly regretfully give up this easy-to-remember and usable name and say, “Alright, Mr. Corpse... uh, spam...” Halfway through, you falter because you simply didn’t pay attention before and only now, after careful recall, do you get the answer.

“Spamton,” he sounds like he’s about ready to give up correcting you completely.

You awkwardly cough twice: “Oh, okay, Spamton. Now I’m notifying you—not asking—so I’m just telling you, you have no choice. I’m taking you back to my home, then once you die... um, to me, you’ll probably serve as reserve food. But don’t worry, I’ll make sure to provide proper end-of-life care!”

Saying this, you clench your fists and swing them a couple of times to psych yourself up, looking quite spirited.

Spamton held back, wanting to argue something more, but if he tried to argue further, he would probably just respond with a hum, then continue repeating these words carelessly.

In the end, he couldn't help but say again: "...... I don't need [hospice care]!! ”

You just spoke in a surprised tone: "Don't you need to?" I think you're almost a corpse. Honestly, I'm pretty sorry you haven't really turned into one...... Oh, if you don't need hospice care, I'll just leave you in the basement and wait to die. But the basement isn't that great—it's not very light-opaque, there's no breathable water, and no proper water supply...... If you're about to die, don't worry so much! To follow the strategy of not doing hospice care, I absolutely won't visit you, nor will I bring you anything. No, I guess the food reserves don't need anything else......

You probably haven’t spoken to a living person for a long time. You babble on since there’s no one to interrupt, unlike before when facing unresponsive things, and since Spamton shows no intent to interrupt, you naturally continue talking.

But he couldn't help but speak, and somehow you could hear a hint of humiliation in his words: "...... I [need][hospice care]. ”

You fell silent regretfully. You thought you could talk a bit more, maybe maybe sell the basement, but you couldn't help but add, "...... Wait, do you really need hospice care? I think the basement environment is pretty good. You're going to die anyway, so staying there isn't a bad idea. ”

"I really [need]. And my [life condition] [good and healthy]. ”

You expressed doubt: "Really?" I just saw you almost dead...... Forget it, since you're saying that, maybe you shouldn't live in the basement. Who knows when the basement will finally welcome a true owner who appreciates it......

Sighing, you finally return home, dragging him along. You open the door and lay Spamton’s head on the bed. With his legs still trailing on the floor, he ends up in an awkward posture.

You clear your throat twice, glance around the slightly messy room, seeing no other options, then place your hands on your hips and announce: “You can stay here! Well, I only have one bed, so you can only lie on this bed. But don’t worry, to prevent leaving your final words unheard in case you suddenly become a corpse, I’ll fully endure the possible stench of your corpse and lie next to you!” You declare solemnly.

“…I’m not going to become a [corpse],” Spamton repeats, sounding exhausted.

“Alright, whatever you say, I need to prepare something to eat first. For proper end-of-life care, I’ll prepare a portion for you too.” You shrug and head into the kitchen.

To be able to eat it yourself, although it's a bit of a pity there are no corpses, maybe changing the method would be okay......

You cut some carrots into a pot, sprinkle some orange peel strings picked out of boredom earlier as seasoning, pour in some rabbit blood stored in the fridge for a day or two, and finally hesitate before adding the whole pigeon you took out that morning—though skinned, probably still has the innards. Then, not really paying attention, you start adding sugar, shaking your hand accidentally, adding a lethal amount of sugar.

Anyway, the steaming hot pigeons with orange, shredded carrot, rabbit, and blood red soup stew are freshly made. When you take a bite, the sweetness mixes with the fishy and bitter flavors. Hmm, actually, the taste is pretty good for you.

Satisfied, you bring the soup out and take two bowls, placing one in front of Spamton.

What Spamton saw was a reddish-blooded soup mixed with an inexplicable mixture, stewing a pigeon.

He stays silent for a moment before asking uncertainly: “…Is this [food]?”

You serve yourself a bowl, happily sipping it, and nod encouragingly at him: “Yes, this is obviously food! Try it, I think it tastes pretty good, and I’m offering proper end-of-life care—you see, I even cooked specially for you…” You even pretend to wipe away fake tears.

In short, you won't hear his answer; you only see him silent for a moment before struggling to speak again: "I am currently [unable to move]." ”

You rub your chin and nod knowingly: "Oh, oh, you need me to finish pretending to feed you, huh...... You also said you wouldn't become a corpse and couldn't even move—this is basically dying. ”

Spamton shuts his mouth, feeling that answering would insult himself because no matter what he explains, you would have your own opinion anyway.

"In short...... This is the [current situation]. "He chose to ignore the issue and continue.

"Alright then, wait until I finish eating. I hope the soup won't get cold by then. I wonder how it will taste if it gets hot......" You muttered to yourself, finally speeding up your pace to finish the soup, ate some pigeon meat, and then refilled a bowl. You even brought over a new spoon, clearly planning to feed it properly.

Watching him drink it, you look at him expectantly: "How does it taste?" ”

Spamton was silent for a moment. He really didn't want to say that he had no sense of taste right now, so he couldn't taste anything. The taste of this soup was ...... At most, it only hurt a little.

But in the end, he gave a definite answer: "The taste is [acceptable]." ”

So you happily wave your fists and give him a thumbs-up, because he can't move right now. You even raise one of his hands and give a high-five.

"Wow, you really have taste! Is this soup really that good? I'll write it down and make it later...... As for the matter of sprinkling too much sugar all at once, just skip it selectively. Anyway, it's still quite tasty now.

“…… Maybe it's not a 'suitable' [recipe]. ”

You grabbed your notebook and happily started writing, and when you heard this, you started muttering, "So what? Anyway, normal methods don't suit me, I just can't eat it...... Oh right, I almost forgot about you. ”

You remember halfway through writing, so you put on disposable gloves, tear off the pigeon meat, and start feeding the corpse of this possibility.

After a while, the soup in the pot did get cold, but the pigeon meat was finished, so you happily went to wash the pots and bowls.

End.
