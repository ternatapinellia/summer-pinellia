Horror Game Port 1 - Old House in the Forest 1 - sptx You

Sptx you, ooc count me

…… Poe got hooked, so the style of this series leans a bit more toward Poe's writing style. Also, since it's a horror game with a genre similar to infinite stream (? ) It's not entirely based on the original work.

——

…… To be honest, you have no idea why you've come here. Just a moment ago, you were drinking beer at a barbecue restaurant, enjoying a tipsy sensation and thinking about what to do next. But the next second, you're sitting here shrouded in darkness, gloomy leaves pressing down overhead, on soft ground where only faint light filters through the gaps between the lush leaves. You casually touch the soft ground, and the first thing you feel is the slightly rough, dry surface of the leaves. Then, with an unintentional press, you hear the crunching sound of the fallen leaves piled together.

You can't just sit and wait for death, so you brush the dust off your clothes and stand up, ignoring the pain in your tailbone from suddenly sitting on the ground. Then you gradually adjust to this drowsy darkness—honestly, you really hope to find a way out so you can leave here quickly, and you don't care what exactly is happening to you, or what is happening beside you. Because you know that it could only mean bad omens, because you have uneasy signs, and you are a selfish person—so you only plan to leave alone.

Unfortunately, just as you recognized the direction of the wind in the forest and planned to follow the wind and step on the creaking leaves, you heard a sound like a pop-up window. In short, it was a bell, and it sounded like an explosion in your mind. You had to stop, almost annoyed, staring at the transparent window before you, which even glowed faintly.

[Main Quest: Escape this forest together with your companions. ]

[Side quest: Uncover the truth behind the 'killer.' ]

You looked at these two lines of text helplessly, so you looked around, then lowered your head and saw another but identical pop-up window—in the subtle dimming, you could barely recognize it as the tiny figure that had been standing nearby before, its outline almost like a puppet. To be honest, at the time you even thought it didn't really exist, after all, this was a forest, and maybe ...... Maybe it was just the incarnation of some tree.

But now, through the screen's light, you can finally see his pale face—or maybe it's his own complexion. He wears a pair of ridiculous pinkish-yellow glasses, so you can't see his mood. Of course, you're not very curious about it. You just crouch down, looking him up and down, and he seems wary, but doesn't say a word. After hesitating for a moment, the puppet finally reaches out his hand to you.

"I think we need to cooperate with [you] and I to leave this [world]." His voice sounded strange, with a crackling sound like electricity. You scratched your ear and felt a bit uncomfortable, but for you, perhaps having another companion in this darkness was actually a blessing—after all, you all saw that panel, and he had no other missions, so perhaps you could let go of your unfounded vigilance and just take this little puppet and escape this forest together.

Of course, as for that side quest, it's probably fine not to complete it...... Just as you were thinking this, you saw the text on that panel update.

[Main Quest: Escape this forest with your companions.] Reward: Survive successfully and have seven days off, immune to all lethal factors

[Side quest: Uncover the truth behind the 'Killer'.] ] Reward: 10,000 gold

You smacked your lips in annoyance. Honestly, this was like suddenly getting involved in some kind of horror game—honestly, ten thousand dark coins made you a bit envious, but you had no deductive skills. You've always believed in physics and cryptography. If you can't unlock it, just break it outright!

So you looked down at the little puppet again, raised your eyebrows, and reached out your hand to him: "Alright, since things have come to this, we can only reach a partnership. But just to be clear, since I can't handle side quests like this kind of deduction and cryptography, I'll leave it to you." You said it as if it were only natural, and without waiting for him to refuse, you continued to add more.

"As for the main quest—don't worry, I'll take you with me and leave here. I don't know how you should address me, but just call me Y/N." You shrugged, watching him reach out his hand toward you. You casually grabbed his hand and shook it, then grabbed it and lifted it up, placing him on your shoulder.

After all, you just heard an unsettling rustling sound from the leaves not far away. It wasn't the sound of wind, but clearer, more obvious human voices. Someone was walking through the woods in this direction, probably just patrolling normally. After all, you had just tried it. The light screen couldn't be broken, so maybe it wasn't because you saw it.

Before he could answer, you heard footsteps getting closer behind you. After all, the sound of stepping on withered leaves was too obvious, not to mention the sound of chainsaws starting the wheels, the sizzling sound, and a faint, terrifying smell of blood—so you just grinned: "Forget it, these things can wait a bit. For now, let's run!" "Better hold on!" ”

With that, you pushed off the ground and dashed out. Honestly, you're quite good at sports, so running a distance is easy—even fast, not difficult. Right now, you're only slightly out of breath, with lactic acid accumulating in your legs and a slight ache. You didn't mind at all, just weaving through the woods, listening to the ominous sound of a chainsaw moving farther away, while you found a more lush, densely packed spot and rushed in.

You were still running, and before Spamton could answer, he was forced to clutch your clothes. Honestly, he didn't want to be thrown off in a place like this—although for the main quest, you'd probably go back to save him, but he didn't want to experience that kind of anxiety. Just as he was about to speak, a gust of wind blew him in, twisting the words he wanted to say and spreading eerie sounds.

"My [name] is—" Unfortunately, the voices behind him were all faded by the wind.

And you just raise your voice and ask, "You—are—saying—what—"

Spamton hated his current situation—unsettling, confusing, and precarious. He always had to come up with a plan to protect himself, always figure out a way to live a better life. Of course, he was willing to endure humiliation and bear heavy burdens, even if it was just a brief experience of being a despised rat, as long as he still had the ability to choose, as long as he had the ability to think, he would never let himself willingly remain in that situation.

And he recognizes you. He knows you are a Citizen of Light he met a few years ago. Unfortunately, he didn't get what he truly wanted from you, but was sent off by money.

But now, relying solely on his own abilities, he couldn't accomplish anything. He was confident that if you hadn't taken him away from that place, he would have been finished, declared his death, and declared it all with deep resentment and displeasure.

If it weren't for you, maybe he wouldn't have ended up in this situation—but without you, he absolutely couldn't have escaped it. If you didn't seem completely unaware, he'd almost think all of this was your deliberate design.

He despised and hated the feeling, but he had no other choice. He could only lie down, clutch your clothes tightly, perform some useless prayers, then close his mouth, saying no more or repeating it.

Only when you can no longer hear the chainsaw behind you do you slowly stop, wipe the unwarranted sweat from your forehead, glance at the Spamton on your shoulder, and feel the weight on your shoulder—you know he's always been there, so you just treat it as a simple burden and run away.

Only after stopping did you realize the trees before you were sparse, even seeming to have a faint glow. You cautiously took a few steps forward, and then everything suddenly opened up—it was actually a small wooden cabin. Surrounded by trees and among the fallen leaves, there was actually a small wooden cabin. It looked somewhat old, but at least with the naked eye, it was clearly not made of new wood, at least some time ago. There was even an iron pot in front of the mansion, but probably no one had used it.

After all, a thin layer of dust had even accumulated on the iron pan, at least two or three months ago.

You hesitated for a moment, touched your belly, then felt it for a moment, looked at Spamton, and said gravely, "...... I think it looks like we need to eat, drink, and rest normally. I just don't know if there are any suitable ingredients left here...... At this moment, you even feel a bit relieved to have discovered such a wooden house. There are probably still candles burning inside, which is why a faint light is emerging.

"Maybe we can take turns keeping watch at night. Also, for the repetitive work, my [name] is Spamton G. Spamton. " said the little puppet.

You nodded and softened your footsteps. After all, there were no fallen leaves near the cabin, so simply keeping your footsteps quiet was fine—you just didn't want anyone to notice your movements, just in case there were others in the cabin.

So you just lowered your voice and asked hopelessly, "Then, maybe you could go in and help me check the situation now?" "After all, you heard something strange coming from inside the house just now. You definitely don't want to end up in the face of a killer after finally coming here. Of course, letting your 'companion' go is no big deal, and he looks small and clearly won't be easily discovered!"

"I think we should [act together]." Your intention was too obvious, after all, you wore that regretful expression on your face right now—as for just now? It was the look of someone racking your brains to plan, so now you have no choice but to regretfully abandon the plan, sigh, and continue walking forward with Spamton.

You climbed the wooden steps and quietly poked your head out from the window to look inside. There was no one there, just candles burning on the table, looking like the wick was about to burn out. You continued to carefully observe the inside of the house, and at that moment, you noticed a heat-insulating cage near the wall, inside which humans were imprisoned—who looked like a Child of Light? You thought this way, since you were also a Citizen of Light, and you had some feelings for your kind. As for how you knew? You had just accidentally entered the Dark World.

And that experience left a bad impression on you—the money you had so hard to bring was swindled away by a slick-talking, smiling salesman!

So now that you remember, turn your head and ask the Spamton on your shoulder: "Uh...... Are you a Citizen of Light or a Citizen of Darkness? ”

Naturally, you didn't recognize that this was the salesperson who had tricked you back then.

You feel as if he glares fiercely at you through his glasses, and you don't care at all, just hoping for an answer from him.

"[People of Darkness]." He didn't hesitate at all, but you always felt his tone carried a hint of gloom. You glanced at him probingly, but couldn't see anything.

So you just scratched your hair and glanced through the window to confirm that the killer was indeed not there, but probably back at any moment. In the end, you climbed in through the window, ignoring the terrified eyes of those inside the cage who then looked at you with pleading eyes—please, you're not that kind, you don't want to save others, and they're probably not real, just characters created by this horror game, so life and death have nothing to do with you. Only after climbing over the window do you realize you could have used the front door.

Then you rummaged through the locker and found a few baguettes, sandwiches that looked like they'd only been stored for a day or two, and of course, some cured meats. In any case, having these foods was already pretty good in this situation. You put Spamton on the table, picked up the food and handed it to him, grabbed a piece of bread yourself, and started wolfing it down.

Spamton didn’t eat, but just looked around the little house, then spoke: '…He is 【raising】 prey, waiting for the 【right moment】 to carry out the 【hunting process】. Meanwhile, these 【prey】, out of fear, are constantly in a 【mentally weakened state】.'

He kindly explained it to you. You slowed your movements a bit, paused, and nodded at Spamton. Of course, you understood what he meant—even though his choice of words was a bit strange, it’s not that you’re dumb, you’re just not good at thinking things through!

'Phew… thanks for the deduction, but for now, let’s just rest. Will you keep watch first?' After eating, you burped and continued to ignore the resentful gazes from the people in the cage behind you.

Spamton nodded, so you grabbed the back of his neck, found where the bedroom was, and then lay down holding Spamton.

You were drowsy, but didn’t forget to remind him: 'Wake me up in the later half of the night; it’ll be my turn to keep watch. Also, if you sense anything unusual, wake me up too.'

Spamton nodded, so you went to sleep peacefully.
