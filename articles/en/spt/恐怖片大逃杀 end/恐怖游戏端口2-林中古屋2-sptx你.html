Horror Game Port 2 - Old House in the Forest 2 - sptx You

…… Poe got hooked, so the style of this series leans a bit more toward Poe's writing style. Also, since it's a horror game with a genre similar to infinite stream (? ) It's not entirely based on the original work. It might be quite long.

——

You slept drowsily in a dark dream world. Calling it completely quiet and peaceful isn't entirely accurate, because after passing through that darkness, you still fell into the dream. To be honest, you almost forgot this memory, but in the dream world, everything remained so clear. Now you looked with some resentment at the salesman in front of you, still dressed in a red suit and smiling, while you had no control over your own actions.

So you know it's just a lucid dream; as long as you watch the process of the dream and wake up again, everything will end. But honestly, to you, it's like a nightmare, so you firmly believe you might wake up before Spamton calls you. After all, having nightmares often means it's hard to have a good sleep experience. But think about where you're falling asleep now...... You won't have so much resentment.

You'd rather encounter more darkness in your dreams, even if it's boundless, just a patch of pitch black that makes you uneasy, even if just a glance makes you shiver uncontrollably, even if, at this very moment, besides this narrow dream street, you stand there with the salesman across from you, and all that's left is darkness.

Alright, since you're here, you can see the other person's lips opening and closing rapidly. You want to hear clearly, to fill in your hazy memories, but no matter how hard you try, you still can't make out clearly. Even if you focus intently, you can only catch the faintest words. You only know that from your impatient tone, you must have made some kind of deal at the time, then you take out your wallet and hand all the coins to the salesperson across from that time. Then comes darkness again. You don't know what happened next—you probably returned to the world of light.

In your dream, you were still trying to recall something, trying to find the vague or lost memories from before. But before you could continue thinking or enjoy a deep, dark sleep, you felt something violently shaking your arm. The crackling sound of electric currents or the unpleasant scraping of wood echoed beside you, sounding like something was being said. So you couldn't help but pull out your pillow and give it a quick blow to the source of the sound.

Now the other person completely quieted down, and your head rested on the bed board. The bed board was quite firm, and if you landed directly on it, it would probably hurt quite a bit. Fortunately, your head wasn't far from the bed board at that moment. Now you were fully awake, quickly picked up the pillow and put it back on the bed, roughly restoring its position.

Then you hear sounds from outside—the door is closed, but the sound is getting closer and closer. You believe that in the next second, that person will be gripping the doorknob. The footsteps have already stopped in front of the door. You can imagine that hand is already resting on it, you hear the clicking sound of the doorknob turning—you swallow hard and instinctively hug Spamton, who seems about to say something, but you cover his mouth. You know your heart is pounding fast; you can almost hear those clear thumping sounds, right beside your ears.

The next moment, he opened the door, and footsteps entered the room. The empty bed was empty. The killer simply scanned the room, then paused for a moment, stopping in front of the bed. He paused for a while, probably observing something, then slowly faded away, just as he had when he arrived—gentle, unhurried.

Lying on the ground, you easily heard amplified footsteps, accompanied by a heartbeat, growing more intense, louder, and more impatient. In that instant, you hugged Spamton tightly and rolled off the bed, instinctively hiding under it—the only place left to hide.

Through the gap in the edge of the bed—or rather, the gap in the hanging sheets—you saw those feet resting not far from the bed, standing for a moment before leaving. You heard the rustling sounds outside, but to be honest, these sounds were drowned out by the pounding of your heart beside you. You almost couldn't hear the sounds outside.

But you know, it was the killer distributing food to those in the cages. Through the gaps in the bed's edge, you could barely see it—you could see the shadow reflected on the sheets, the elongated reflection on the ground—then you watched the man raise the axe and bring it down hard. You clutched Spamton's shoulder and shivered instinctively. Then you heard the sound of blades hitting the cutting board, smelled roughly the scent of blood. You thought, it was probably chopping meat.

The process was almost torturous. You tensed your nerves, and the drowsiness you still felt faded away at that moment. Honestly, if you weren't afraid of accidents, you'd wish you could sleep a little longer. So you lowered your head to look at Spamton, who also looked up at you. But in reality, even if you looked at each other, it was just staring at each other—after all, you couldn't tell what was hidden in each other's eyes.

After a while, you hear footsteps fading away, hear a loud door slamming, and guess the killer is gone. So you peek out through the gap in the bed's edge. You no longer see that long reflection, only the countless cages cast shadows on the floor. Finally, you climb out from under the bed with Spamton, letting go of the hand covering his mouth.

You were still a bit timid and guilty, after all, the people inside the cage outside saw you two walk into the room openly. How could nothing happen? But when you look outside, all you can see are those people's angry or innocent eyes. You don't know what to ask, and in the end, Spamton tugged at your sleeve: "I [believe] [we] should hurry up [time] to search for [clues]." ”

Only then did you realize what was happening, and hurriedly nodded: "You're right...... Anyway, we've already reached his bedroom. Let's first see what clues are inside." ”

After saying that, you chose to check the nightstand first. Naturally, aside from an old calendar and a photo frame placed by the bedside, there was nothing else. You felt a bit discouraged, but instinctively glanced at the photo frame—you knew the person in the center was probably the killer who had left in a hurry, since the person in the middle looked a bit inexperienced. But the two people beside him were completely blacked out, making it impossible to tell their backgrounds.

Frowning, you finally remembered the contents of the photo. Then you waved for Spamton to come over and look at it. Seeing him turning his back, seemingly studying something on the bookshelf, he was even standing on tiptoe because he couldn't reach it, so you simply called out to him.

"Come over here and take a look, Spamton." Anyway, you don't plan to leave the decryption to yourself; you're just lazy...... Besides, it seems your companion is pretty smart, so it's fine to use it, right? You thought this with a clear conscience, then saw him walk over and casually stand up to make room for him.

But when Spamton arrived, he handed you a newspaper. You glanced at it—it was from October 11, 1923—honestly, the year was way too far from you. You instinctively glanced at the calendar again and saw that today was April 5, 1945.

You exhale a sigh of relief. A newspaper that has been around for 22 years still appears here, and apart from the paper's inherent wrinkles and yellowing from age, there are no visible signs of damage, not even dust. This is itself strange.

He shook the newspaper, and to his surprise, this well-preserved old paper only had two pages left. You see, Spamton was still studying the bedside table, so he decided to check the contents of the newspaper first.

[The xxx couple suddenly had an accident in Honshu. The crime scene was in a local forest. The bodies of the two were hard to identify, with flesh and blood mixed together. Their bodies were located in the trunk of an RV. Witnesses estimated that they had been dead for more than twenty-four hours when they found them, but the exact time of death is difficult to determine. It is speculated that the two were attacked while traveling to Honshu while having a picnic in the woods......]

You glanced at the date and instinctively frowned, endured the discomfort to finish reading the report, including the black-and-white photo of the corpse printed on the front page, then turned the page, intending to see what else was written in the newspaper.

Turning to the next page, you instinctively glance at the heading of the question, only to find that the date has changed. It was originally 1923, but now the header is now June 13, 1925.

You muttered to yourself, barely recalling that this day happened to be Black Friday. It was all mysterious, and you didn't really care about it. You just glanced at the report.

[Shocking: The xxx couple once jointly cheated on another couple. The husband treated the other woman's affair as a partner, and the wife in turn treated the other husband as the affair partner!] This incident has stirred up a two-year uproar in this prefecture. The verdict regarding the perpetrator's violent acts has been difficult to determine, and public opinion calls for lenient sentences. According to relevant laws, murder is punishable by life imprisonment or even the death penalty......]

…… What kind of mess is this? If you still want to complain, you shake the newspaper and realize it's probably a photocopy, because behind this page is the content printed on the back of another newspaper dated another day.

By now, Spamton had finished thinking about the nightstand and walked toward the bookshelf. Once again, though probably out of reach, Spamton called out to you: "I need your [help]." ”

You quickly responded with a couple of 'ohs' and walked over to him. You didn't have time to read the newspaper carefully, only glancing at the headline and the approximate date—it was November 10, 1932.

As for the content...... It seems that under public pressure, after nine years of trial and overturning the case, the court finally decided to give a lighter sentence and release the couple after twelve years of detention.

You don't understand why the court is pressured by public opinion—probably some damn jury bench system. As long as you believe someone is innocent, after the trial of 12 people, they can be acquitted...... These 12 people were randomly selected from the public.

And you just casually put down the newspaper, hugged the spamton, and lifted it up.

Only then did you notice there was a puzzle mechanism on top of the bookshelf. It was basically a numeric mechanism engraved on books—you had no idea how it actually worked. You noticed that the patterns carved into the book were gibberish numbers.

01111239, whether to say or not, even ranked from childhood to adulthood, you were momentarily speechless.

"I think the [key] is the [date] of the [newspaper]." At that moment, Spamton interrupts your thoughts.

You snapped back to reality and let out a couple of 'oh-oh' sounds. Actually, you wanted to touch your chin and pretend to be deep, but holding Spamton made it impossible for you to do that, so you just shrugged: "Then tell me, which date should it be?" ”

I glanced at the password arrangement that could match this date, but there were two possible combinations. You didn't want to make a mistake, so I decided to ask Spamton decisively.

You feel the little puppet in your hand seem speechless for a second, then patiently explains: "I guess the [blacked-out figure] in the [photo] represents his [parents], and being blacked out means [not being recognized] or [should not exist.] [Death] is true [separation], while the pressure of [public opinion] and the [perpetrator's] [escape] represent the true [departure] of [parents] to him. ”

You understood and nodded, pondering: 'So... it was on the day that the murderous couple got a light sentence, uh-huh.' Then you realized, so you began to move the books, more precisely, to change the characters on the books. Just as you had changed the previous number to 1923—you heard footsteps from outside, you heard the sound of the door handle turning, after all, your ears are very sensitive. You instinctively glanced back, thankfully, the door could just block you and Spamton; at this moment, you were standing in front of the bookshelf, right in the blind spot. But unfortunately, you didn't know what was behind the bookshelf, didn't know where the end of the password led, and naturally didn't dare to take a risk. Moreover, this side just happened to have no window, so you couldn't hide to the side either—

So in the end, you gritted your teeth, rolled with Spamton right there, and hid under the bed.

You held your breath, and you felt once again that you could hear your heartbeat clearly. You heard the footsteps slowly approaching, as if slowly passing a death sentence. You saw that long shadow stop in front of the bookshelf, saw his footsteps. Your breathing became rapid, but you only dared to cover your nose—the twisted number hadn't been restored yet—and in the room, the timely hiding place was probably only under the bed. At this critical moment, you realized this, but at that moment it was already too late.

Because in the next second, your eyes met a pair of clear blue eyes, your breath froze, you could clearly see the red veins in the whites of his eyes, and you could easily recognize the contempt and rage in that gaze. Instinctively, you first stuffed the Spamton you were holding into your clothes, thinking that the killer probably hadn't noticed him.
