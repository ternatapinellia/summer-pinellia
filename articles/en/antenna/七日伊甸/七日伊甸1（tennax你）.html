Seven Days Eden 1 (tennax you)

Tennax you. This article won't be long.

Comparing personal art. The inspiration came from the seventh day of Hua Zang and Yu Hua. I used my own writing style from seven years ago.

——

You are the undertaker.

…… Or rather, you are the owner of this funeral home called Bi'an. In fact, from the boss to the employees, you are the one. Because most of the people who worked before you have died, flowers bloom all over your body, growing from your eye sockets, reproducing from your bones, crowding your blood vessels and meridians, penetrating your skin, and blooming with bright, blood-red flowers, which have taken over the heat and scent that once belonged to humans, replaced by a strange, bloody scent tinged with floral scent.

This phenomenon is when the flower seeds hidden within them finally bloom, and the flower blooming disease has come to an end. This phenomenon is called 'blooming.' Once it blooms, it withers immediately. The funeral home you work at is specifically for those who have flower blooming disease. As for the task...... It is to fulfill the wishes of those customers before they fully bloom.

And now, after fulfilling the last customer's wish, you're about to die. You know, this is your deadline. There are fewer and fewer new arrivals, and you hear that the flower bloom disease is gradually being cured, with no new cases this year. So you plan to close the store completely after completing this order.

As for your flower...... It is a poppy growing in the socket of your right eye. The flower has been eating away at your eyeball, but now that socket is empty, you feel the plant's roots and stems are about to spread to your brain.

Ding dong, the wind chime at the door rang—a new mission had arrived.

You walk out of the shop and help the porter bring in a new body—no, calling it a corpse might not be accurate, but surprisingly, it's twice the height of a regular customer. You kneel beside this corpse. Although it's not that you don't welcome ordinary customers, ordinary people are not sent to you, but rather to another funeral home for cremation.

But you still gently lift the white cloth and push aside the pure white chrysanthemums surrounding you.

Pitch-black and shattered screens, broken wires, bent antennas, missing arms. This is a TV transformed into a dark citizen, and you get that impression.

So, first, let's get to the stage. You haven't really managed the machine, but you still have a rough idea of how to do it.

So, you fetch a basin of water, wet a towel, gently wipe the dust off the screen, and then reveal your exposed body.

Then, you brought insulating tape and scissors, straightened the bent antenna, and applied the tape. On both sides of the white cloth lay his broken arm. You carefully reconnected the wires of the corresponding color and size, compared them to the relatively intact and clear cuts, taped each one on, and finally gently put the arm back.

Second...... You glanced outside the shop, and the porter left after that, so probably no one mourned him. So you went inside, took a piece of black gauze, cut it into a piece, and wrapped it around your arm as a tribute. After that, you could prepare for the burial.

You walked out of the room carrying your pristine white burial clothes, surprised to see the TV person actually sitting up from the stage. He was so tall, almost reaching the ceiling, the pitch-black screen was empty, just those shattered screens...... No, there were only a few cracks. You had just applied a few simple layers of insulation tape, so the black screen still had white tape.

Looking down, you find that at the joint of the broken arm, a flower had somehow sprouted—an iris, a blue irise. So the broken arm didn't fall off, since you connected the wires yourself, and the newly grown flower was responsible for connecting the metal sheets together.

Blink gently, you know, this is your last guest. But...... You don't know how long it will take for it to bloom.

Then you see that pure white chrysanthemum sprouting at the junction. So you realize it will only last about seven days.

…… Seven days is exactly the time you have estimated yourself to bloom. Then, everything is still in time.

So, you stepped forward, reached out your hand to the television, which was probably still a bit dazed from your own awakening, and said calmly, "In seven days, you'll be dead. Before that, do you have any wishes you haven't fulfilled? I will help you fulfill them." ”

That TV head turned to look at you: 'Oh, although I don’t exactly know what’s going on, I am Mr.(ant)tenna, and, it’s very nice to meet you——' His voice abruptly stopped. He spoke very quickly, looking extremely anxious, probably still not having processed the meaning of your words.

Then, his voice faltered, and the screen turned into a distorted screen.

You just look at him calmly, your hand still there: "Well, nice to meet you, Mr. Tenna. Is there a wish you need me to help you fulfill?" ”

"Oh—I don't have any wishes, and I don't need your help—" He sounded provoked by those words, his screen flickering, his speech still fast, even a bit sharp.

And you just quietly watched him, without any emotion, hearing that Tenna finally had something to do and abruptly stopped, then cautiously and fearfully asked, "...... Is it really only seven days?" ”

You nodded, glanced again at the pathology report that had fallen onto the white cloth, which you had just overlooked, and confirmed it, then spoke firmly: "Yes, seven days, sir." ”

The pitch-black screen was silent. You watched him get out of bed and stand in front of you with a sense of pressure, so you subtly took two steps back.

"So, do you have any wishes? These are the last seven days." You said.

Tenna was silent for a moment, her tone softening, or maybe ...... Did you hear a hint of deathly silence in it? You think, yes, it's hard to accept when you learn you only have seven days to live.

"So...... My wish today is for you to come watch my show." His tone was relaxed, or perhaps a bit trembling, but at this moment, he was still trying hard to act as if nothing had happened.

It seemed as if everything was going smoothly, resting peacefully.

You lift your head slightly, look at him, and nod: "Is this your only wish?" Well then, please come this way, sir. "Go in another direction; it's more spacious over there.

You hear Tenna's voice behind you: "No, that's just today's wish." ”

You're not surprised at all. Sometimes, guests arriving at the other shore may have multiple wishes, but in any case, you will do your best to fulfill them.

So you nod: "No problem—then, save tomorrow's wishes for tomorrow, Mr. Tenna." ”

You sit on the sofa, have Tenna sit at the low counter across from you, watch him hold the TV head, and then start the show. You rest your hands on your knees, quietly watching the show.

To be honest, it's a bit clichéd—no, actually, it's outdated. You understand why no one else comes to mourn him, but at least for now, as the owner of a funeral home, of course you mourn someone who is about to die.

You turn off the lights, and in the darkness, only the light from the TV screen remains. Now playing a movie called "Flowers at the End of the Road," which tells the story of two women fleeing on the highway after committing crimes. The film's light flickers on your face, skipping over the old program channels from earlier, and finally you stop at this movie.

Maybe you just want to enjoy quiet viewing for a longer time. At least for now, none of you have spoken, and no other voices have been made—only the sound of the movie comes through the tape-clad machine.

And the movie footage is slightly obscured by the tape you just applied. Of course, you don't really care about that.

Until the movie finished, you turned the lights back on, and you watched as Tenna's screen turned completely black again.

You heard him speak, his words tinged with nervousness, probably nervousness and weakness: "So, do you like the video I just played?" ”

You thought for a moment and said, "Very good." ”

"Oh! So, do you want to watch other films? Choose whichever you want......" He seemed to get flustered again, speaking very quickly.

You interrupted him, calmly looking at the screen: "Mr. Tenna, anything is fine, as long as you're happy. Now it's your wish to come true." ”

He stopped abruptly, and after a while spoke again: "...... So, I wonder, is this the Huakai disease? I've heard of it. How far are you from your death? ”

"Seven days, Mr. Tenna."

“…… Is no one fulfilling your wishes? ”

"No one, Mr. Tenna."

"So, what about your wish?"

"I have no wishes, sir."

“…… How could people not have desires? ”

You hear him muttering.

But you just tilted your head and calmly said, "So, do you have any wishes to fulfill today?" ”

"My wish?" Oh...... Today, right now, at this very moment, my wish is that you can be a little happier because of my show...... In short, I want to fulfill your wish right now. ”

You blink slowly, your gaze flickering at that moment, so you nod: "...... Alright, I will grant your wish. Well then, please continue the show. ”

Sitting back in your original seat, you watch the game show playing on his screen, and it just so happens you have a game controller too. It's just that the game content is a bit old, lacking anything new, and even treats the quiz game as part of a mini-game. Since there aren't any real questions, it's just simple questions about each person's name.

But despite the age, your mood was very calm. It wasn't particularly interesting, but at that moment, your mood calmed down, so you gently tugged at the corner of your mouth and gave a faint smile...... If you had to describe it in a word, it would be the pure white lily.

You say, "I'm a bit happier, Mr. Tenna." ”

Unconsciously, the sky darkened, and the day was coming to an end.

And so the first day ended.
